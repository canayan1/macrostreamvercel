FX Blotter Analytics Beta

Analyze the FX blotter your desk already keeps. Drop in the export as it is. Kalibre detects the columns, matches deals into positions and shows where the book made and lost money.

The first version works. Open the sample Excel blotter to see the result, or download the input and result files from the demo page. We are looking for a small number of desks to try it on their own blotters. Opportunity cost is not shown yet; it will be added once its definition is agreed with desks.

What it reads

The goal is to read the export without reformatting it:

  • CSV and Excel exports from systems such as 360T, FXall, Bloomberg FXGO and Murex
  • Excel blotters kept by hand, with English, Turkish, German or French headers
  • Spot, outright forwards, swap legs and NDFs. Options are detected and left out.

Trade Date · Value Date · Currency Pair · Buy/Sell · Notional · Rate · Counterparty · Book

How columns are detected

Headers and values

Headers are matched against English and Turkish synonyms, and values by their patterns: pairs, dates, amounts and rates. In a "bought currency and amount, sold currency and amount" layout, the rate is derived from the two amounts.

You confirm

Before import, Kalibre shows what it found, for example "Trade date = column C". Blotters usually record the side from the bank's view; that is the default, and one choice switches it. Rates are read as all-in rates and value dates as dates. Rows that cannot be read are listed with a reason.

What you see

Built around how a treasury desk reads its day:

  • Daily P&L: the day's mark-to-market plus realized P&L. Realized P&L is recognized on the value date.
  • Spot P&L and swap-points P&L on separate lines
  • Carry cost shown as overnight swap
  • Client margin from the blotter's cost column
  • Intraday net open position (NOP) by currency and on a value-date ladder
  • Amounts in USD equivalent first, then in local currency
  • One-click drill-down from any figure to the deals behind it

For marking and currency equivalents you supply an end-of-day or intraday rates file. We have no market data licence.

The standard

What a head of treasury trusts is a fast table that is never wrong. That is the acceptance bar for the first version: every total traces back to deal rows, 12 hand-computed cases match exactly, and a reconciliation panel with 7 checks runs on every file.

How data is handled

  • The file is read in your browser and is not uploaded to our servers.
  • Counterparty names are dropped by default. Deal IDs are used only to match legs and positions.
  • Demos run on synthetic or anonymized data. Do not send real client data.
