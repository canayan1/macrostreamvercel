Methodology

You should know where every number in the lab comes from and how it is calculated. This page covers the data, the rules of the quick test and its known limits.

Where the data comes from

Kalibre does not publish market data. Every series in the lab comes from one of three sources, named on the series:

SourceLabelNotes
Sample seriesSYNTHETICGenerated by a seeded random process. It does not show a real market; it is for getting to know the lab.

Figures in the (Turkish) education articles are taken once from public sources, with source and date in the article, and are not updated.

Candle times

Candle times from MT5 are your broker's server time, the time you see on the MT5 chart. We do not convert them to UTC: converting past candles with today's offset would shift them by an hour around daylight-saving changes. The lab shows the server time's offset from UTC separately.

How the quick test works

The quick test mimics the rules of the Kalibre engine in MetaTrader 5, in your browser. Same as the engine:

  • Rules are evaluated at the open of a new candle, using the last closed candle. Crossover rules use the last two candles. A future candle is never used.
  • Exit rules are evaluated before entry rules. A signal in the opposite direction closes the open position.
  • If both directions signal on the same candle, no trade is opened.
  • Lot size comes from balance, risk per trade, the volatility scale and the stop distance.

Approximations:

  • The order of prices inside a candle is unknown. If both stop and target are touched in the same candle, the stop counts; this is a cautious assumption.
  • The trailing stop is updated at candle close.
  • No spread, commission, swap or slippage. No news filter.
So the quick test is for ruling an idea out, not for confirming it. The final test is the same rule file in MT5 Strategy Tester, with your broker's cost settings.

Volatility models

The volatility lab offers three measures. EWMA weights recent returns exponentially (the RiskMetrics approach). Yang-Zhang uses open, close and intraday range together. GARCH models volatility clustering and its return to a long-run level; its parameters are estimated on your data by maximum likelihood.

How to read results

  • Always read return together with the largest drawdown. A rule that earns the same with half the drawdown is the sturdier one.
  • With few trades, the result may be luck. A rule that won with a handful of trades is not evidence.
  • Trying a rule with many settings and keeping the best one is curve fitting. Re-test the setting you like on a period you did not test.
  • A rule that worked in the past may not work in the future.
