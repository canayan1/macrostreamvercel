Why trading data needs an analytics layer.

Every trade is recorded somewhere. Yet simple questions about those records still take a day of spreadsheet work.

Blotters live in spreadsheets

A dealing desk keeps its deals in an Excel blotter exported from another system. Reports are rebuilt by hand, and every rebuild can break.

P&L that cannot be traced

A daily figure gets reported, but the split between spot, swap points and carry is hard to show. When someone questions a number, finding the deals behind it is slow.

Limits checked by hand

Position and loss limits are often checked after the fact, by someone reading a sheet. A breach can be found late.

We sit between the trade record and the decision. We do not sit between a client and the market.

Who we work with.

We start with traders, because they can test software on their own history today. Our long-term work is with institutions.

Active and prop traders

People who trade their own or a funded account and want to know which habits cost them money. They are the first to test our engine, on their own files.

Where we start

Brokers and prop firms

Firms that run many accounts and need the same rules applied to each one. They need analytics and rule monitoring per account, not one trader at a time.

Where we grow

Bank and corporate treasury desks

Desks that run FX positions and report P&L to a treasurer and a risk team. They need numbers that trace back to each deal.

What we believe.

Four principles decide what we build and what we leave out.

Check the number before you show it

Our engine is compared trade by trade against the MetaTrader 5 Strategy Tester. A separate reference implementation, written without access to the engine code, recomputes the same rules. Neither check left an unexplained difference.

Read the methodology

Data stays on your side

Files are processed in your browser and are not uploaded. Live MT5 data is encrypted end to end with AES-256, and the relay deletes it within 2 hours. We never ask for a password.

How we work with institutions.

A desk should be able to judge the software before any client data leaves the building. Our pilots are designed around that.

  1. Start on sample or anonymized data

    We begin with our sample files or an anonymized export of your own. No real client data is needed to judge the fit.

  2. Your data stays with you

    Files are read in the browser on your own machine. We do not receive or store your blotter.

  3. Bring your own rate files

    Valuation uses the rates you already have. We do not supply market data, so there is no new market data licence to sign.

  4. Talk to the founder

    Questions and requests go to the founder, not a sales team. What we learn in a pilot shapes what we build next.

We are looking for treasury desks, brokers and prop firms to run early pilots with. Tell us your role and the system your data comes from.
